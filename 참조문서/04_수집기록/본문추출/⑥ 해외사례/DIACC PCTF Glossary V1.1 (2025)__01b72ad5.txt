1. Scope 
The Pan-Canadian Trust Framework (PCTF) Glossary provides definitions and 
examples for terms that appear across DIACC PCTF documentation. The objective of 
the PCTF Glossary is to ensure all stakeholders have a shared and consistent 
understanding of terms used in the context of the PCTF. As terms and usage can vary 
across industry, the Glossary is recommended reading for anyone wanting a strong 
baseline understanding of the PCTF. The Glossary may also be of use in linking the 
various elements of PCTF documentation to each other where their use of any Terms is 
related.  
The structure and content of the PCTF Glossary items is: 
 
Term (Translation) 
A definition statement that provides the accepted and precise meaning of the 
associated term in the PCTF context. 
• Examples: Examples may be included to help clarify the intended meaning of a 
term; the examples provided are not intended to be an exhaustive list. 
• Reference: Reference(s) may be added to indicate where the term and definition 
is used in other PCTF documentation. 
See also: Links to similar meaning or contextually related Terms defined elsewhere in 
the Glossary. 
Notes: 
• The terms included for the current version of the Glossary are those used across 
the PCTF. 
• Within the Glossary definitions, terms that are capitalized refer to Glossary 
definitions of that term, which may differ from their everyday English meaning. 
• The list of Glossary terms has been kept to just the essentials. 
• Terms with the same or similar meanings are collapsed to a single entry but may 
be expanded upon in source PCTF documentation. 
• Terms used with their everyday, English dictionary meaning (e.g., stakeholders) 
are not included.

2. Conventions and Guidelines 
The PCTF Glossary is a living document that will evolve as the framework is developed. 
The guidelines for creating definitions for the terms in the Glossary are: 
1. The definition of a term should reflect the information-mapping methodology for 
defining concepts. The definition should clearly indicate the larger category to 
which the concept belongs, and the critical attributes or characteristics of that 
concept that distinguish it from others; 
2. The meaning of the term should reflect the current usage of the term in a PCTF 
document; and 
3. Consult existing digital identity standards or frameworks as sources for 
definitions, with preference being given to Canadian sources.  
As a result of guidelines 1 and 2, most existing definitions could not be taken verbatim, 
but would need to be modified (e.g., change person to Subject) to be considered a valid 
definition in the context of the PCTF. 
3. Terms and Definitions 
Adaptive Risk (Risque adaptatif) 
A dynamic measure of the risk associated with a transaction or service access based on 
context and behaviour. 
• Example: adjust the security measures for online banking based on the user’s 
location and recent activity, ensuring higher scrutiny for unusual access patterns 
• Reference: Authentication 
Adaptive Risk Authentication (Authentification du risque adaptatif) 
Dynamically adjusting the specific authentication steps performed according to the 
Adaptive Risk. 
• Example: when a banking app prompts a user for additional verification, such as 
a fingerprint or security question, after detecting a login attempt from a new 
device or unfamiliar location.

Applicant (Demandeur) 
An Applicant is any Entity that has requested, though not yet received, a Credential. 
• Example: a Person who has requested, though not yet received, a drivers' 
license from a province or territory. 
• Reference: Digital Wallet, Credentials, and Infrastructure

Assigned Identifier (Identifiant attribué) 
Letters, numbers, symbols, or a combination thereof that a Responsible Authority 
allocates to an Organization and which can be used to uniquely identify the 
Organization within a given context, use, or system. 
• Example: a unique alphanumeric code allocated by a provincial and/or federal 
regulatory authority to identify an organization within government digital services 
systems. 
• Reference: Privacy and Verified Organization 
Attestation (Attestation) 
A trusted verification of something as true or authentic. 
• Example: when an employer digitally verifies and signs a document confirming 
an employee’s credentials, such as a professional certification, which is then 
used to access industry-specific systems or services. 
• Reference: Digital Wallet 
• See also: Claim, Attribute

Attribute Definition (Définition de l’attribut) 
An Attribute Definition is a Credential that describes a specific type, or class, of 
Attribute. An Attribute Definition does not describe a specific instance of an Attribute 
(e.g., Martina’s specific date of birth; Hiren’s specific degree). Rather, the Attribute 
Definition describes the characteristics of such Attributes. 
• Example: specifying a user's residential address as a necessary attribute for 
verifying eligibility to access region-specific online government services, such as 
applying for a local driver's license. 
• Reference: Credentials 
• See also: Attribute 
Authentication (Authentification) 
The process of establishing truth or genuineness to generate an assurance that a 
Subject has control over an Issued Authentication Credential and that the Authentication 
Credential is currently valid. 
• Example: when a user logs into their online banking account by entering a 
password and then confirming their identity through a one-time code sent to their 
registered mobile device. 
• Reference: Authentication (Credentials, Trust Registries, Digital Wallet, 
Infrastructure, Notice and Consent)

• Example: a security token that generates a unique, time-based code used in

Authentication Factors (Authentication Factors) 
There are three Authentication Factors: 
1. Something the Subject has (e.g., key card, key fob)  
2. Something the Subject knows (e.g., password)  
3. Something the Subject is or does (e.g., a biometric)  
• Example: using a combination of something you have (a smartphone with an 
authentication app), something you know (a password), and something you are 
(a fingerprint scan) to securely access a sensitive online account. 
• Reference: Authentication 
• See also: Credential 
Authentication Service Provider (Fournisseur de services 
d'authentification) 
An Entity that operates a service that implements the Authentication Trusted Processes 
related to authentication: 
1. Authentication 
2. Authentication Session Initiation (optional) 
3. Authentication Session Termination (optional) 
• Example: Google, with their services like Google Sign-In, allowing Users to 
authenticate themselves across various websites and applications using their 
Google account credentials. 
• Reference: Authentication 
• See also: Identity Provider 
Authenticator (Authentificateur) 
Information or biometric characteristics under the control of an individual that is a 
specific instance of an Authenticator Type; the specific instance of the Authenticator 
Type that is under the control of the individual.  
An Authenticator may be provided by the Subject or by a service provider. The term 
may also commonly refer to a physical device or application.

Authoritative Party (Partie qui fait autorité) 
A Role that a Participant performs to provide Identity Information or Identity Evidence at 
a Level of Assurance to Relying Parties. 
• Examples: a bank; government department of immigration; government driver's 
licence program; a business registry; a telecommunications company; 
government-issued identity card. 
• Reference: Credentials, Verified Person 
• See also: Identity Provider and Disclosing Organization  
Authoritative Source (Source qui fait autorité) 
A collection or registry of identity records maintained by an Authoritative Party that 
meets the PCTF Conformance Criteria for establishing evidence of identity. 
• Examples: vital statistics register; Verified Person record; business registry; bank 
account record. 
• Reference: Credentials, Digital Wallet, Verified Organization, Verified Person 
Authorized Agent (Agent autorisé) 
Any Entity providing services related to Verified Organization on behalf of a Responsible 
Authority through a formal relationship. 
• Example: a third-party service provider that handles customer verification and 
account management for a bank under a formal agreement with the bank’s 
regulatory authority. 
• Reference: Verified Organization 
• See also: Responsible Authority 
Authorized Personnel (Personnel autorisé) 
Staff of a Responsible Authority assigned to perform certain tasks. Typically, employees 
of or persons working under contract for the Responsible Authority.

Consent (Consentement) 
Permission, given from a User authorized to do so, to share Identity and/or Personal 
Information about a Subject as per the terms defined in a Notice. In the context of the 
PCTF, consent is equated to "Meaningful Consent" as described by the Office of the 
Privacy Commissioner of Canada.  
Note: Some public sector organizations have the legislated authority to collect, use, 
disclose, update, retain, and store personal information in the execution of their 
functions. In these cases, public sector organizations provide notice to the person, but 
they do not require the person’s consent.  Consent requirements for each jurisdiction's 
legislation must be adhered to.  
• Example: agreeing to share home address information to a service provider. 
• Reference: Credentials, Digital Wallet, Notice & Consent, Privacy, Trust 
Registries 
• See also: EULA 
 
Consent Decision (Décision de consentement) 
The decision by the Subject to provide consent or decline consent. 
• Example: the User click’s the Agree button at the end of EULA dialog or clicking 
‘Accept Cookies’ on a website page. 
• Reference: Notice & Consent, Privacy 
Contextual Evidence of Identity (Preuve d’identité contextuelle) 
Evidence of Identity that establishes the existence and Digital Representations of 
Entities within a specific context and for a specific purpose. 
• Examples: bank account; health record; provincially issued driver's licence; 
Canadian passport; business account with a telco; better business bureau 
record; government-issued identity card used as evidence in the process of 
verifying an individual's identity. 
• Reference: Verified Person 
• See also: Authorized Agent, Reliable Authority

Contextual Evidence of Organizational Identity (Preuve contextuelle 
de l’identité organisationnelle) 
Information providing evidence of Organizational Identity is usually tied to program 
administration or service delivery activities. Created by private sector Entities and public 
sector Entities. Contextual Evidence of Organizational Identity may corroborate 
Foundational Evidence of Organizational Identity and may include information beyond 
Organizational Identity Information (e.g., mailing address). This information may be 
used to assist in linking Organizational Identity Information across jurisdictions and 
services.  
• Examples: CRA BN registration document, municipal business permit, a DUNS 
number, used as evidence in the process of verifying an Organization’s identity. 
• Reference: Verified Organization 
Contextual Identity Record (Dossier d’identité contextuelle) 
A record that provides Contextual Evidence of Organizational Identity. 
• Example: a business's tax registration document, which serves as contextual 
evidence of the organization's legal identity when applying for a government 
contract. 
• Reference: Verified Organization 
• See also: Contextual Evidence of Organizational Identity 
Credential (Justificatif) 
A data structure that uniquely binds at least one Authenticator to at least one claim 
about at least one Subject. 
• Examples: the Subject is licensed to drive; the Subject resides at a specified 
address; the Subject has a specific certification. 
• Reference: Authentication, Credentials, Trust Registries and Digital Wallet 
• See also: Authenticator, Subject, Verifiable Credential

Credential Issuance (Délivrance des justificatifs) 
A process during which a Credential is issued, describing one or more Subjects, and 
bound to one or more appropriate Authenticators controlled by the Holder. A Credential 
includes one or more identifiers which may be pseudonymous and may contain 
attributes verified by the Credential issuer. The Authenticators may be issued during this 
process, provided by the Subject or provided by a third party. The bound Authenticators 
will be subsequently used to prove, at a Level of Assurance, that a Credential is 
referring to the same Subject that was originally bound to the Authentication Credential. 
• Example: when a university issues a digital diploma to a graduate, it includes the 
student's unique identifier and verified academic achievements, binding it to the 
student's authentication method for future verification. 
• Reference: Authentication 
• See also: Authenticator, Attributes, Credential, Subject 
Credential Maintenance (Maintenance des justificatifs) 
Credential Maintenance process includes life-cycle activities such as binding new 
Authenticators, removing Authenticators, and updating Authenticators (e.g., password 
change, updating security questions and answers), or updating Authentication 
Credential attributes.  
This process is typically initiated by a User but may also be initiated by a system 
administrator or automatically by the system. 
• Example: when a user updates their email password and security questions in an 
online banking account, ensuring the authentication credentials remain current 
and secure. 
• Reference: Authentication 
Credential Recovery (Récupération des justificatifs) 
The Credential Recovery process provides a means to transition an Inaccessible 
Credential to an Issued Credential. The process may be triggered by a User, system 
administrator, or automatically by the system. This process is optional and may not be 
supported by all service providers.

• Example: when a user resets a forgotten password through an email verification

Credential Revocation (Révocation des justificatifs) 
The Credential Revocation process ensures that a Credential is permanently disabled 
or deleted. Once a Credential is revoked, it can no longer be used. The system will 
actively prevent further Trusted Processes from occurring in relation to this Credential. 
The process can be initiated by a User, system administrator, or automatically by the 
system.  
Note that a new Credential can be issued for the same Subject. Re-issue equates to 
revoking a Credential and issuing a new Credential for the same Subject. 
• Example: when an employee's company access card is permanently disabled by 
the system after their employment ends, preventing any further use of the card 
for building access. 
• Reference: Authentication 
Credential Service Provider (Fournisseur de services de justificatifs) 
An Entity that operates a service that implements the Authentication Trusted Processes 
related to management of Credentials: 
1. Credential Issuance 
2. Credential Suspension (optional) 
3. Credential Recovery (optional) 
4. Credential Maintenance 
5. Credential Revocation 
• Example: an organization which manages the issuance, maintenance, and 
revocation of digital credentials for employees to securely access corporate 
systems. 
• Reference: Authentication

Credential Suspension (Suspension des justificatifs) 
A process that converts an Issued Credential to an Inaccessible Credential and may be 
initiated by User action, system administrator, or automatically by the system.  
An Inaccessible Credential should not be used in the Authentication process. 
• Example: when a bank temporarily disables a customer's online banking 
credentials after detecting suspicious activity, preventing access until the issue is 
resolved. 
• Reference: Authentication
