Executive Summary 
The EU-Japan Interoperability Pilot has succeeded, demonstrating the feasibility of cross-border exchange of 
learning credentials between European Union Digital Identity (EUDI) and Japanese environment built for this 
pilot project.  
 
This pilot project explored the technical requirements and implementation challenges for the interoperability of 
digital credential. As a result, the EU and Japan (hereinafter referred to as "both sides") confirmed that the 
issuance, presentation, and verification of credentials can be implemented by utilizing Verifiable Credentials 
(VCs), Digital Identity Wallet (DIW), and e-Seals. 
 
Key Achievements include: 
• 
Successfully implemented cross-border credential presentation, issuance, and verification scenarios. 
• 
Demonstrated interoperability between EU and Japanese environment for this pilot project.  
• 
Organized technical challenges, including the alignment of interpretations of technical specifications 
and validation methodologies. 
• 
Established and validated a shared understanding on implementation method of selective disclosure 
for secure digital credential exchange. Established that the core technology stack can handle 
international interoperability requirements while both sides have different governance frameworks—
such as laws, regulations, and certification system of trust services—and different technical 
architectures. This demonstration of tangible progress toward international digital credential 
ecosystems builds stakeholder confidence in the viability of cross-border digital identity solutions and 
creates a foundation for future policy and implementation decisions. 
Limitations and Future Work: 
This pilot focused primarily on technical interoperability validation.  
 Key areas for future development include: 
• 
Alignment of signature algorithms and certificate chains 
• 
Exploring mechanisms for mutual reference of multiple trusted lists 
Purpose 
The overarching purpose of this document is to formally record the successful execution of an interoperability 
pilot of verifiable credentials and digital identity wallets between the EU and Japan.  
This initiative specifically targeted the seamless issuance, presentation and verification of learning credentials 
between the European Digital Identity Wallet prototype and a Japanese wallet, establishing a verified 
benchmark for functional performance.  
As background of this pilot project, both sides carried out the implementation of the Memorandum of 
Cooperation on Digital Identities and Trust Services signed in April 2024 to contribute to the implementation of 
Data Free Flow with Trust and welcomed the progress made towards concrete use cases.  
In this context, both sides selected conducting the pilot project as a concrete step of collaborative actions in 
the implementation of the MoC, in order to test the technical interoperability of relevant system, while ensuring 
that an exchanged document can be trusted, verified, and accepted across borders without administrative 
burdens or delays. 
This report serves as a definitive validation that all underlying technical architectures and interfaces have met, 
or exceeded, the rigorous predefined benchmarks for data integrity, cryptographic security, and the fluid

Project Overview 
Outline of the Pilot Project 
The MoC aims to facilitate Data Free Flow with Trust between the two regions. This agreement is part of the EU-
Japan Digital Partnership, which focuses on advancing cooperation on digital issues through different 
mechanisms. 
The MoC promotes secure and reliable digital interactions among citizens, businesses, and public authorities, 
supporting economic growth and innovation. It also aims at enhancing international trade, electronic 
commerce, and the delivery of essential services. 
Both sides acknowledge the importance of international cooperation and the need for advancing towards a 
global trust framework that may allow, in the future, international agreements or other appropriate legal 
instruments for possible mutual recognition of identity management systems. 
The project represents a collaborative effort to enhance digital connectivity. The initiative was designed to verify 
the alignment of the respective technical and governance frameworks and check their capability to support an 
interoperable environment. 
The testing was performed within a controlled environment designed to replicate a real-world scenario. The 
EUDIW prototype was used to interact with issuing and verifying relying parties created by Japan in a sandbox 
environment, while the Japanese wallet interacted with issuing and verifying relying parties in the EU. 
 
Scope 
The interoperability of credentials was tested to confirm that they could be appropriately issued, stored, 
presented, and verified across different countries and regions. Academic credentials were used as the use case 
scenario in this pilot project. 
Both sides prepared wallet infrastructures based on or interoperable with the Architectural Reference 
Framework (ARF) of the EUDIW to share and store verifiable credentials as well as trusted lists as a verification 
function, and carried out issuance, storage, presentation, and verification of credentials within a sandbox 
environment.  
To ensure the authenticity of the credentials, Japan and the EU used e‑Seals provided by accredited service 
providers under their respective frameworks, or data with equivalent functions. 
During the pilot, issuance of Japanese credentials to an EUDIW prototype and vice versa was demonstrated. 
Also, presentation of credentials stored in the wallets, independently of the provider of those wallets, against 
relying parties from both sides was confirmed. 
The sandbox environment was implemented for this pilot project in accordance with international or regional 
data model and protocols, while some minor adjustments were made, by mutual agreement, when it was 
difficult for either side to meet the conditions. 
The pilot focused on learning credentials, which are records of learning outcomes acquired by a learner 
following a small volume of learning. Therefore, learning credentials entail those academic credentials that 
show the learner has specific knowledge, skills and competences that respond to societal, personal, cultural 
or labour market needs.

The pilot supported the following presentation scenarios: 
• 
A Japanese student, using a Japanese wallet, presents a learning credential to a European verifier (e.g., 
a university). 
• 
A European student, using an EUDI wallet, presents a learning credential to a Japanese verifier (e.g., a 
university). 
This pilot supported the following issuance scenarios: 
• 
A Japanese student is issued to its Japanese wallet a learning credential from a European issuer  
• 
A European student is issued to its EUDI wallet a learning credential from a Japanese issuer   
Technical Architecture 
A high-level overview of technical architecture is presented in this section.  
 
Learning Credential Data Model 
The pilot defined a comprehensive data model for learning credentials including: 
• 
Basic student identity information (family name, given name) supporting selective disclosure 
functionality 
• 
Achievement details and learning outcomes 
• 
Institutional accreditation information 
• 
Quality assurance indicators 
Standards and Protocols 
The implementation leveraged established technical standards and protocols to ensure interoperability 
between EUDI and Japanese environment built for the pilot project: 
• 
OpenID for Verifiable Presentations (OpenID4VP) v1, used for credential presentation 
• 
OpenID for Verifiable Credential Issuance (OpenID4VCI) v1, used for credential issuance 
• 
Both OpenID protocols were profiled by OpenID4VC High Assurance Interoperability Profile (HAIP) v1.0 
• 
IETF SD-JWT VC format for the encoding of the learning credential attestation and for privacy-preserving 
selective disclosure of its attributes 
• 
ETSI TS 119 602 v1.1.1 standard for the data model of lists of trusted entities   
• 
Certification scheme for e‑Seal  of the Ministry of Internal Affairs and Communications (MIC) of Japan 
Security Framework 
To ensure secure and trustworthy cross-border credential exchange, the pilot implemented a security 
framework encompassing: 
• 
Cryptographic Standards: EC P-256 keys with ES256 signatures and RSA signatures 
• 
Trust Model: a combination of out-of-band knowledge of the certificates of the trusted entities & two 
lists of trusted entities (certificates exchanged between the parties) 
• 
Certificate-based trust establishment with multiple trusted lists for different purposes 
• 
Privacy Protection: Selective disclosure enabling minimal data sharing 
• 
Key Binding: Cryptographic proof of possession

Comprehensive test logs, video documentation, and system evidence were captured for all successful test 
scenarios.

Phase 1: Cross-Border Presentation 
The tests for Phase 1 of the pilot were executed by both parties on 15 December 2025. 
Both presentation scenarios were successfully validated: 
• 
Japanese verifier ↔ EU wallet. 
• 
EU verifier ↔ Japanese wallet. 
 
Verification areas: 
• 
Cross-device presentation flows 
• 
Selective disclosure functionality 
In this 1st phase, the trust between the parties involved was implemented by configuring the services and 
wallets with out-of-band knowledge of the issuers/verifiers' certificates based on OpenID4VP v1 and HAIP v1.0 
and not by using any trusted lists.  
The 2nd phase was designed in order to place particular importance on the establishment of trusted lists and 
the development of functionalities related to OID4VCI. With the purpose of establishing trusted lists as a 
dynamic and machine-readable mechanism that enables verification by third countries.  
Phase 2: Cross-Border Issuance & Presentation 
The tests Phase 2 of the pilot were executed by both parties in February 2026. 
The main purpose of these tests was to demonstrate cross-border issuance of Learning Credentials between 
European Union (EU) and Japanese (JP) Wallets and Issuers. Additionally, cross-border presentation of Learning 
Credentials between EU and JP Wallets and Verifiers was also demonstrated.

Conclusion 
The EU-Japan Digital Credential Interoperability Pilot has succeeded, demonstrating that cross-border digital 
credential exchange can be achieved using established international standards between countries or regions 
with different technical and regulatory frameworks. The pilot has shown that the technical framework for secure, 
privacy-preserving credential presentation and issuance is viable for cross-border scenarios. 
The completion of all planned test scenarios and the resolution of technical issues encountered during testing 
provide valuable insights for future implementations. The validation of OpenID4VP, OpenID4VCI, and SD-JWT 
VC standards in an international context contributes to the broader understanding of digital credential 
interoperability requirements. 
While this pilot focused primarily on technical validation, it contributes to a foundation for future policy and 
implementation decisions. Key areas requiring further development include the implementation of digital 
identities using verifiable credentials and digital identity wallets in various fields across a wide range of sectors.  
As shown in our Joint Statement, both sides welcome the successful completion of this pilot project clearly 
demonstrates that the interoperability of digital credentials can be ensured even between countries or regions 
under different governance frameworks—such as laws, regulations, and certification system of trust services—
and different technical architectures. The pilot project demonstrated that international interoperability can be 
ensured through existing architectures, even if parties have the different governance frameworks.
